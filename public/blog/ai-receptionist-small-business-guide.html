AI Receptionists for Small Businesses: What They Do and How to Set One Up

By ARKA · Published November 25, 2026

The short answer: An AI receptionist is an assistant that handles first contact with customers: it answers common questions from information you approve, captures enquiry details, books appointments and hands anything complex or sensitive to a person. For a small business, the main benefit is that every enquiry gets a fast, accurate first response, including evenings and weekends.

What an AI receptionist actually does

The term covers a range of tools, from a scripted chat widget to a fully integrated assistant. A useful AI receptionist usually does four jobs:

  • Answers questions about services, hours, locations, process and policies, using information you have approved rather than general knowledge.
  • Captures enquiries by asking for the details your team needs (name, contact details, what they need, timing) and logging them in your CRM or inbox.
  • Books time by checking real availability in your calendar or booking system and confirming the slot.
  • Hands off to a person whenever a question is sensitive, complicated or outside what it has been told it can answer.

Channels vary. Website chat and email are the most common starting points. Text messaging and voice depend on your phone provider and the platforms involved, so they are usually scoped separately.

Where it helps most

AI receptionists pay off where enquiries are frequent, time-sensitive and similar to each other:

  • Service businesses such as cleaning, renovation and detailing, where quote requests arrive at all hours.
  • Clinics and practices, for questions about services, locations and booking, while clinical questions go to staff.
  • Real estate teams, where the first agent to respond to a new lead often wins the conversation.
  • Professional firms, to triage new enquiries and book consultations without a back-and-forth email chain.

It helps least where every conversation is unique, highly sensitive or depends on judgement from the first message.

Where it doesn't belong

An AI receptionist should not give medical, legal or financial advice, quote prices it hasn't been given, or make commitments on your behalf. It should not pretend to be human. The safest setups state that the assistant is automated, keep it to approved information, and route uncertain questions to a person rather than letting the model guess.

How to set one up responsibly

  1. List the top questions. Pull the 20 to 30 questions your team answers most often from your inbox and messages.
  2. Write the approved answers. These become the assistant's source of truth. If an answer changes, update it here first.
  3. Define the hand-off rules. Decide which topics always go to a person and how quickly someone responds.
  4. Connect the systems. Calendar, CRM and inbox, so bookings and leads are recorded automatically.
  5. Test with real questions before launch, including awkward ones, and review conversations regularly afterwards.

Privacy and messaging rules in Canada

Conversations can include personal information, so the usual privacy obligations apply. In Ontario, private-sector businesses are generally covered by PIPEDA; clinics may also have obligations under PHIPA. Document what the assistant collects, where it is stored and who can access it.

If the assistant sends follow-up emails or texts to prospects, those are likely commercial electronic messages under Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL), which requires consent, sender identification and an unsubscribe mechanism.

This is general information, not legal advice. See the Office of the Privacy Commissioner and the CRTC's CASL guidance for authoritative information.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an AI receptionist the same as a chatbot?

A basic chatbot follows a fixed script. An AI receptionist is set up around your real services, availability and policies, can book appointments and log leads, and knows when to hand the conversation to a person.

Can an AI receptionist answer phone calls?

Some can, depending on the phone provider and platforms involved. Website chat and email are the usual starting point, and voice is scoped separately.

Should customers be told they're talking to AI?

Yes. Being clear that the assistant is automated, and offering a person on request, protects trust and avoids misleading customers.

What does it need to work well?

Approved answers to your most common questions, clear hand-off rules, and connections to your calendar and CRM.
