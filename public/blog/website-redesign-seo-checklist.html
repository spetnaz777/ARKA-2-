Website Redesign SEO Checklist: How to Redesign Without Losing Rankings

By ARKA · Published November 25, 2026

The short answer: Most traffic lost in a redesign comes from URLs that change without redirects, content that disappears, and metadata or structured data left behind. Inventory every existing URL before you start, keep or improve the content that earns traffic, set permanent (301) redirects for anything that moves, and check indexing in Search Console after launch.

Before the redesign: take an inventory

  • Export every URL from your sitemap and a crawl of the current site.
  • Record what earns traffic: top pages and queries from Google Search Console, plus pages that other sites link to.
  • Save titles, meta descriptions and headings for each important page.
  • Note structured data already in place (LocalBusiness, Product, FAQ, Article and so on).

During the redesign: protect what works

  • Keep URLs the same wherever possible. Changing structure for its own sake costs visibility.
  • Keep or improve the content on pages that rank. A beautiful page with less information often ranks worse.
  • Merge weak or duplicate pages deliberately, redirecting each one to the most relevant new page.
  • Build a redirect map: every old URL to one new URL, using permanent (301 or 308) redirects. Avoid redirecting everything to the homepage.
  • Carry over or improve titles, descriptions, headings, image alt text and structured data.
  • Keep the staging site out of search: password protection or noindex, removed at launch.

Launch day

  1. Remove any noindex or password protection from the live site.
  2. Deploy the redirects and test a sample of old URLs, especially the top traffic pages.
  3. Submit the new XML sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  4. Check robots.txt isn't blocking important sections.
  5. Confirm analytics and conversion tracking still fire.

The first month after launch

  • Watch Search Console for crawl errors, 404s and pages dropping out of the index.
  • Use URL Inspection to request indexing for the most important new pages.
  • Compare traffic and enquiries to the same period before launch, page by page.
  • Fix broken internal links and add redirects for any old URLs you missed.

Some fluctuation in the first weeks is normal while search engines recrawl. Sustained drops usually point to missing redirects or removed content.

Don't forget accessibility and speed

A redesign is the best time to fix accessibility and performance. Ontario's AODA references WCAG 2.0 Level AA for organizations it covers, and faster, mobile-friendly pages tend to convert better for everyone. See our AODA guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will redesigning my website hurt SEO?

It can if URLs change without redirects or valuable content is removed. With a URL inventory, a complete redirect map and content kept or improved, rankings usually carry over.

Should I change my URLs during a redesign?

Only when there's a clear benefit. Every changed URL needs a permanent redirect, and some short-term fluctuation is normal.

How long until rankings recover after a redesign?

Often a few weeks if redirects and content are handled well. Longer drops usually indicate missing redirects or removed content.

Do I need to resubmit my sitemap?

Yes. Submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools at launch and check for errors.
