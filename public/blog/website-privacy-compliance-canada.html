Website Privacy Basics for Canadian Businesses: Forms, Email and Tracking

By ARKA · Published November 25, 2026

The short answer: If your website collects personal information through contact forms, bookings or tracking, Canadian privacy law applies. For most private-sector businesses that means PIPEDA: say what you collect and why, collect only what you need, protect it and let people access or correct it. Marketing emails and texts also fall under CASL, which requires consent and an unsubscribe option.

Which laws usually apply

  • PIPEDA, Canada's federal private-sector privacy law, applies to personal information collected in the course of commercial activity in most provinces, including Ontario.
  • Provincial laws apply in some cases: Quebec, Alberta and British Columbia have their own private-sector privacy laws, and Ontario health information custodians have obligations under PHIPA.
  • CASL, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation, covers commercial electronic messages such as marketing emails and texts.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. For your obligations, see the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and the CRTC's CASL guidance, or consult a lawyer.

Contact and booking forms

  • Ask only for what you need to respond. Most enquiry forms need a name, an email or phone number and a message.
  • Say briefly, next to the form, what the information is used for and link to your privacy policy.
  • Make sure submissions arrive somewhere secure and that only the right people can see them.
  • Don't collect sensitive information (health details, ID numbers) through a general contact form.

A privacy policy people can read

Your privacy policy should explain, in plain language: what you collect, why, which service providers process it (hosting, email delivery, analytics), whether it is stored outside Canada, how long you keep it, and how someone can ask to see, correct or delete their information. Name a contact for privacy questions.

Email follow-ups and CASL

Replying to someone who contacted you is different from adding them to marketing. Under CASL, commercial electronic messages generally need express or implied consent, must identify the sender and must include a working unsubscribe mechanism. Keep a record of how and when consent was obtained.

Analytics, cookies and tracking

Analytics and advertising tools collect information about visitors' behaviour and devices. Disclose the tools you use in your privacy policy, consider privacy-friendly analytics where they meet your needs, and remove tracking scripts you no longer use. Quebec's law has specific requirements for technologies that identify, locate or profile people, which matters if you serve Quebec residents.

Security basics

  • Serve the whole site over HTTPS.
  • Use security headers and keep software and plugins updated.
  • Protect forms against spam and abuse with honeypots and rate limiting rather than collecting more data.
  • Limit who can access submissions and turn on two-factor authentication for admin accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a small business website need a privacy policy?

If the site collects personal information, such as through a contact form, it is good practice and generally expected under PIPEDA's openness principle to explain how that information is handled.

Can I add contact form submissions to my newsletter?

Not automatically. Under CASL, marketing messages need consent. Ask separately with an unticked checkbox and keep a record of the consent.

Does PIPEDA apply if my website host is in the US?

PIPEDA applies to your handling of personal information regardless of where your provider is located, and storing data outside Canada should be disclosed in your privacy policy.

Do I need a cookie banner in Canada?

Canadian law doesn't use the EU's cookie-banner model, but you must be open about tracking and obtain appropriate consent. Quebec has more specific rules.
